Cloud of the Month

Cumulus Mediocris

Cumulus mediocris

— Dr. Stratus Nimbus, Head of Acquisitions

Classification: Low-level convective cloud
Altitude: 600-2,000 meters (2,000-6,500 feet)
Formation: Thermal convection in stable atmosphere
Appearance: Moderate vertical development with crisp edges

"This month, we celebrate the humble Cumulus mediocris. Often overlooked in favor of its more dramatic cousins, the 'mediocris' represents a state of perfect atmospheric equilibrium. Note the crisp, cauliflower-like outlines and the flat, dark base. It's not showing off, it's just... being. A lesson for us all."

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this website related to Apple iCloud? +

No. We are concerned with the aqueous, not the aqueous silicon. Theirs is a brand. Ours is a calling.

Do you have an app? +

We encourage our members to experience the sky directly, without a digital intermediary. Look up. Your phone is in your hand.

My cloud evaporated before I could photograph it. Can I still report it? +

The transient nature of clouds is what makes them beautiful. We accept post-evaporation testimonials, but they are subject to a higher standard of narrative excellence.

Are contrails considered clouds? +

This is a topic of heated debate in our forums. The purists say no, the progressives say yes. We encourage you to join and form your own opinion.
